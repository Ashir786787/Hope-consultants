# destinations — 2

## Poland

Tagline: Lower tuition and living costs, growing global recognition of degrees, and strong post-study work options.

### Overview
Poland offers international students a genuine bargain: well-established universities, tuition and living costs well below Western Europe, and degrees whose recognition is growing fast across the EU. It is an excellent choice for students who want a real European experience on a budget, and its post-study work options are far more open than most people assume.

### Intakes
- Main: October
- Secondary: February (programme-dependent)

### Study costs (approximate, annual, PLN)
- Public university tuition: PLN 10,000 – 20,000
- Living costs: PLN 2,500 – 4,000 per month

### Work rights
- Part-time work allowed while studying
- No permit required for students with valid study status (with limits)

### Post-study work
Graduates can apply for a temporary residence permit to work after completing their studies. EU-wide recognition of Polish degrees continues to improve, and many graduates enter the wider EU labour market.

### Visa route
- Level of visa: National Visa (Type D)
- Proof of funds required: Yes
- Health insurance required: Yes
- Interview: Sometimes required
- Typical processing: 4–8 weeks

### Housing
Student dormitories are affordable and widely available, and private rentals in university towns are inexpensive compared to the West. Arranging housing several weeks before arrival is recommended.

### Scholarships
NAWA scholarships appear in 04-scholarships. Many Polish universities also offer tuition discounts or waivers to strong international applicants.

### Is this for you?
Poland suits students who want a recognised European education at a low cost, and who are willing to manage a less structured system with real independence.

## Malaysia

Tagline: Affordable degrees with international campuses, booming tech hubs, and a low cost of living for students.

### Overview
Malaysia has built one of Asia's strongest international education systems, with foreign university branch campuses, English-taught programmes, and a cost of living far below most of the West. Its tech and services economy is booming, and its degrees carry genuine international recognition.

### Intakes
- Main: September
- Secondary: January, May

### Study costs (approximate, annual, MYR)
- Public university tuition: MYR 15,000 – 35,000
- Living costs: MYR 1,500 – 2,500 per month

### Work rights
- Part-time work allowed in approved sectors while studying

### Post-study work
Graduates can apply for a work permit through employers. Malaysia's growing technology and business hubs consistently hire international graduates with strong English and technical skills.

### Visa route
- Level of visa: Student Pass (VISAS)
- Proof of funds required: Yes
- Health insurance required: Yes
- Interview: Not usually required
- Typical processing: 4–8 weeks

### Housing
University accommodation and private apartments are both affordable. Most international students live in student residences or shared apartments near campus.

### Scholarships
Malaysian Government Scholarships appear in 04-scholarships. Universities also offer merit-based tuition waivers for international students.

### Is this for you?
Malaysia suits students seeking affordable, English-taught, internationally recognised programmes in a growing economy, especially in business, engineering and technology.

## USA

Tagline: The widest range of universities on earth, powerful alumni networks, and STEM pathways to long-term work and residence.

### Overview
The United States hosts more universities, more disciplines and more alumni networks than any country on earth. Admission is holistic rather than score-only, which opens doors for well-rounded students. STEM graduates enjoy extended work periods under OPT, and the visa interview, while demanding, is a skill you can prepare for with the right guidance.

### Intakes
- Main: Fall (August/September)
- Secondary: Spring (January) — many programmes only admit in Fall

### Study costs (approximate, annual, USD)
- Public university tuition: USD 25,000 – 45,000
- Private university tuition: USD 40,000 – 60,000
- Living costs: USD 12,000 – 18,000

### Work rights
- On-campus work allowed (up to 20 hours/week during term)
- Off-campus work generally requires special approval (Curricular/Pre-completion OPT)

### Post-study work
STEM graduates can work up to 36 months total under OPT extensions. Employers can then sponsor H-1B visas, which is the main path to longer-term work. Non-STEM graduates typically have 12 months.

### Visa route
- Level of visa: Student Visa (F-1)
- Proof of funds required: Yes
- Health insurance required: Yes (mostly waived on-campus plans)
- Interview: Required
- Typical processing: 2–8 weeks

### Housing
On-campus residence halls are standard for first-year students. Off-campus apartments require early planning, especially in university towns.

### Scholarships
Fulbright appears in 04-scholarships. US universities award significant merit and financial aid packages directly at admission, making the sticker price misleading without an offer.

### Is this for you?
The USA suits ambitious, well-rounded students who want the widest possible choice of universities, disciplines and professional networks, and who can prepare seriously for the F-1 visa interview.

## Denmark

Tagline: Free tuition for many EU students, English-taught degrees, and a top-ranked tech and design ecosystem.

### Overview
Denmark combines free tuition for EU/EEA students, a genuinely English-first academic environment, and one of the world's strongest tech and design ecosystems. Non-EU students pay tuition but benefit from a highly ranked, innovative system where English-taught programmes are the norm rather than the exception. Copenhagen in particular is a magnet for global tech talent.

### Intakes
- Main: September
- Secondary: February (for some programmes)

### Study costs (approximate, annual, DKK)
- Public university tuition (non-EU): DKK 45,000 – 120,000
- Living costs: DKK 9,000 – 12,000 per month

### Work rights
- Part-time work allowed while studying
- Students can work without a permit during study periods

### Post-study work
The Establishment Card grants up to three years to seek employment after graduation. Extended post-study schemes exist for specific categories, and Danish employers sponsor international talent readily.

### Visa route
- Level of visa: Residence Permit (Study)
- Proof of funds required: Yes
- Health insurance required: Yes
- Interview: Not usually required
- Typical processing: 2–4 months

### Housing
University accommodation (kollegier) is subsidised but competitive; private rentals are expensive, especially in Copenhagen. Apply for housing the day course admission arrives.

### Scholarships
Danish Government Scholarships cover partial tuition, as listed in 04-scholarships. They are awarded by universities directly.

### Is this for you?
Denmark suits students aiming at tech, design, architecture and engineering in a high-cost, high-quality, English-first environment, with strong post-study pathways.

## Netherlands

Tagline: English-first country with internationally ranked universities and a culture built for international graduates.

### Overview
The Netherlands runs the most genuinely international higher education system in Europe: hundreds of English-taught programmes, universities that rank among the world's best, and a culture that treats international graduates as an ordinary part of the workforce. The country's open, pragmatic society makes entry and settling genuinely smooth for students who come as part of an academic programme.

### Intakes
- Main: September
- Secondary: February (for many programmes)

### Study costs (approximate, annual, EUR)
- Public university tuition: EUR 8,000 – 20,000
- Living costs: EUR 1,000 – 1,500 per month

### Work rights
- Part-time work allowed (up to 16 hours/week during study, more in holidays)

### Post-study work
The orientation year (zoekjaar) gives graduates up to one year (two with a research career) to seek a job or launch a business. Successful job seekers convert to Highly Skilled Migrant status.

### Visa route
- Level of visa: Student Visa (MVV + VVR)
- Proof of funds required: Yes
- Health insurance required: Yes
- Interview: Not usually required
- Typical processing: 4–8 weeks

### Housing
Dutch housing is famously competitive, especially in Amsterdam. University housing services exist for internationals; secure your place before arrival.

### Scholarships
The Orange Knowledge Programme appears in 04-scholarships. Universities award additional merit scholarships at admission, and Dutch degrees are well recognised in the EU job market.

### Is this for you?
The Netherlands suits students who want an English-first, internationally oriented education with strong post-study opportunities and a pragmatic, well-organised system.

## Norway

Tagline: Free public university tuition, a purely English-friendly teaching environment, and one of the best quality-of-life records on earth.

### Overview
Norway offers free tuition at public universities for all students — including internationals — paid for by the state. English is used so widely that international students function fully from day one. The high cost of living is the real investment, but one of the world's strongest job markets, safest societies, and fair economic models make it a remarkable choice.

### Intakes
- Main: August/September
- Secondary: Limited; most programmes admit only in the Fall

### Study costs (approximate, annual, NOK)
- Public university tuition: 0 (some programmes charge fees)
- Living costs: NOK 12,000 – 16,000 per month

### Work rights
- Students can work part-time (up to 20 hours/week) while studying

### Post-study work
The job-seeker residence permit lets graduates stay up to one year to find work. Norwegian employers hire international talent readily in tech, engineering, energy and health, which feeds into permanent residence.

### Visa route
- Level of visa: Residence Permit (Study)
- Proof of funds required: Yes (blocked account)
- Health insurance required: Partial (national insurance for residents)
- Interview: Not usually required
- Typical processing: 8–16 weeks

### Housing
SiO/SiT student housing is subsidised and priority is given to international students, but supply is limited. Private rentals are expensive; apply for housing as soon as you can.

### Scholarships
The historical Quota Scheme has ceased for new students (see 04-scholarships). Universities now offer limited merit scholarships instead.

### Is this for you?
Norway suits students who want free, high-quality public education, a simply run system, and high quality of life, and who can plan a realistic budget around Norway's living costs.

## Sweden

Tagline: Innovation and design capital of the north, English-taught programmes, and a green, sustainable lifestyle.

### Overview
Sweden is where the world's best design, innovation and sustainability meet higher education. It pioneered English-taught master's programmes in Europe, its universities collaborate constantly with global companies in Stockholm and Gothenburg, and a green, safe, bike-friendly society wraps it all together.

### Intakes
- Main: Autumn (late August)
- Secondary: Spring (January), for some master's programmes

### Study costs (approximate, annual, SEK)
- Public university tuition (non-EU): SEK 80,000 – 180,000
- Living costs: SEK 9,000 – 13,000 per month

### Work rights
- Students can work part-time while studying; no separate permit for study periods

### Post-study work
Students with a permit can stay up to one year after graduation to seek employment. An approved job leads to a work permit that counts toward permanent residence. Swedish employers view local degrees as a strong signal.

### Visa route
- Level of visa: Residence Permit (Study)
- Proof of funds required: Yes
- Health insurance required: Yes
- Interview: Not usually required
- Typical processing: 2–4 months

### Housing
Swedish student housing is subsidised and run through local foundations; supply is short in Stockholm and Gothenburg. Apply the day you accept your admission.

### Scholarships
Swedish Institute Scholarships appear in 04-scholarships and cover full tuition plus stipend. Many universities also offer merit-based tuition waivers for international students.

### Is this for you?
Sweden suits students with a clear programme vision — especially in design, tech, business and sustainability — who value innovation, a green lifestyle, and generous post-study work pathways that build towards residence.
