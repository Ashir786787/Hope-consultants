# destinations — 1

## New Zealand

Tagline: NZQF accredited qualifications, strong post-study work rights, and a clear pathway to residence. Study once, settle for good.

### Overview
New Zealand runs a genuinely simple system for international students who want to study, work and eventually settle. Qualifications sit under the NZQF framework, which makes comparing courses and levels straightforward, and post-study work rights are among the most generous anywhere. The country is safe, small, and modern — with a quality of life most students describe as the biggest surprise of their time abroad.

### Intakes
- Main: February (Semester 1)
- Secondary: July (Semester 2)
- Some programmes: November/December short intakes

### Study costs (approximate, annual, NZD)
- Public university tuition: NZD 25,000 – 45,000
- Living costs: NZD 20,000 – 25,000

### Work rights
- On-campus work: allowed while studying
- Up to 20 hours/week during study terms
- Full-time during official holidays

### Post-study work
Students who complete a qualifying qualification under the NZQF can apply for a Post-Study Work Visa. Its length depends on the level and location of the study, and it can lead to a residence pathway when the qualification and job align with the Skilled Migrant Category.

### Visa route
- Level of visa: Student Visa (Fee-Paying)
- Proof of funds required: Yes
- Health insurance required: Yes
- Interview: Not usually required
- Typical processing: 6–12 weeks

### Housing
Student hostels, homestays and flatting are all common. On-campus accommodation is limited in smaller towns, so most students plan housing early. Homestay is a strong first choice for 17–19 year olds.

### Scholarships
New Zealand International Scholarships run once a year and are highly competitive; they are listed in 04-scholarships. Individual universities also offer automatic partial scholarships based on grades.

### Is this for you?
New Zealand suits students who want a smaller, safer, English-speaking country with a planned pathway to residence. The system rewards candidates who are organised and start early.

## Australia

Tagline: World-class universities, summer-style living, and generous post-study work rights with a range of PR pathway options.

### Overview
Australia is the most popular study destination among Pakistani students for good reason: strongly ranked universities, a warm and familiar culture, and a genuinely clear work-and-residence ladder. The country runs three major intakes, which makes planning easier, and its post-study work rights were expanded in recent years for longer value from your qualification.

### Intakes
- Main: February
- Secondary: July
- Additional: November (less common, programme-dependent)

### Study costs (approximate, annual, AUD)
- Public university tuition: AUD 30,000 – 50,000
- Living costs: AUD 24,000 – 30,000

### Work rights
- Up to 48 hours/fortnightly limit during study
- Full-time (two-week) work rights during scheduled breaks

### Post-study work
Temporary Graduate Visa (subclass 485) provides post-study work rights after qualifying study. Longer post-study work periods are available for regional study and certain qualification levels, and skilled PR options exist via the General Skilled Migration programme.

### Visa route
- Level of visa: Student Visa (subclass 500)
- Proof of funds required: Yes
- Health insurance required: Yes
- Interview: Not usually required
- Typical processing: 2–8 weeks

### Housing
University-run residences, purpose-built student accommodation and shared flats dominate the city experience. Search early — November and December bookings are the safest.

### Scholarships
Australia Awards are the flagship route and appear in 04-scholarships. Individual universities also offer automatic partial scholarships at admission that reduce tuition without a separate application.

### Is this for you?
Australia suits students who want a big, familiar, English-speaking system, strong universities, and a clear, long-term residence pathway. It rewards early planning and honest budgeting.

## Canada

Tagline: Semester-based admissions, strong work rights for students, and one of the most student-friendly PR systems in the world.

### Overview
Canada runs semester-based admissions, which means a wider range of start dates than most English-speaking countries. It combines strong universities, a genuinely multicultural society, and one of the most student-friendly permanent residence systems anywhere. Its work rights during and after study are designed to turn international graduates into residents, which makes it a first choice for many families.

### Intakes
- Main: Fall (September)
- Secondary: Winter (January), Summer (May) — programme-dependent

### Study costs (approximate, annual, CAD)
- Public university tuition: CAD 25,000 – 45,000
- Living costs: CAD 15,000 – 20,000

### Work rights
- Up to 20 hours/week during academic terms
- Full-time during scheduled breaks

### Post-study work
Post-Graduation Work Permit (PGWP) matches the length of study, letting graduates accumulate the Canadian work experience required for the Express Entry PR system. This is the closest thing to a planned residence pathway in the English-speaking world.

### Visa route
- Level of visa: Study Permit (with Temporary Resident Visa/Electronic Travel Authorization)
- Proof of funds required: Yes
- Health insurance required: Yes (province-dependent)
- Interview: Usually not required
- Typical processing: 4–12 weeks

### Housing
Purpose-built student residences, apartments and shared houses are available in every major city. On-campus housing fills fast for the Fall intake — apply right after admission.

### Scholarships
Canada's flagship international scholarships sit in 04-scholarships (for example IDRC Research Awards). Many universities also offer entrance scholarships awarded automatically by grades.

### Is this for you?
Canada suits students who want predictable admissions, honest budgeting and a real pathway to permanent residence. It rewards students who are ready to study hard and work part-time with a plan.

## United Kingdom

Tagline: Shorter one-year master's programmes, a global academic reputation, and a strong graduate route for work after study.

### Overview
The United Kingdom is the historic home of English-language higher education. Its one-year master's degrees cut a year of cost and time compared with most systems, and the Graduate Route gives qualifying graduates two years of work rights after study. British qualifications carry weight everywhere, and the academic infrastructure is almost unmatched.

### Intakes
- Main: September/October
- Secondary: January (for programmes that offer it)
- Some: May/June short intakes

### Study costs (approximate, annual, GBP)
- Public university tuition: GBP 20,000 – 30,000 (UG), GBP 15,000 – 25,000 (PG)
- Living costs: GBP 12,000 – 18,000

### Work rights
- Up to 20 hours/week during term time (depending on the visa route)
- Full-time during holidays

### Post-study work
The Graduate Route allows graduates of eligible courses to work for two years (three for PhD) without a sponsor. This often feeds into Skilled Worker sponsorship, which is the main long-term work route.

### Visa route
- Level of visa: Student Visa (Route)
- Proof of funds required: Yes
- Health insurance required: Yes (Immigration Health Surcharge)
- Interview: Usually not required
- Typical processing: 3–6 weeks

### Housing
University halls of residence are the standard for first-year and one-year master's students. Private student housing is strong in every university city; book during the summer for September starts.

### Scholarships
Commonwealth Scholarships appear in 04-scholarships. Universities also offer merit scholarships and discounts, often automatic at admission.

### Is this for you?
The UK suits students who want a globally recognised qualification in a shorter, denser timeframe, and who want access to a strong post-study work route. It suits fast, intense students who plan early.

## Ireland

Tagline: Home to Europe's tech giants, moderate tuition, and a two-year post-study work visa that many students convert into residence.

### Overview
Ireland combines the English language of the US and UK with Europe's friendliest tech economy. Tuition is moderate compared to the US/UK, and the two-year Third Level Graduate Scheme gives graduates room to work and convert to residence. For students aiming at tech, finance or pharma careers, Ireland is a sleeper destination worth a serious look.

### Intakes
- Main: September
- Secondary: January (for some postgraduate courses)

### Study costs (approximate, annual, EUR)
- Public university tuition: EUR 15,000 – 25,000
- Living costs: EUR 12,000 – 16,000

### Work rights
- Up to 20 hours/week during term time
- Full-time during vacation

### Post-study work
The Third Level Graduate Scheme grants graduates two years to find employment. Work on a General Employment Permit or Critical Skills Employment Permit then becomes the route to long-term residence and, eventually, citizenship.

### Visa route
- Level of visa: Student Visa
- Proof of funds required: Yes
- Health insurance required: Yes
- Interview: Sometimes required for certain profiles
- Typical processing: 4–8 weeks

### Housing
Student accommodation is the biggest practical challenge in Dublin; search earliest in cities. Submit your housing application at the same time as your visa documents.

### Scholarships
Ireland Government Scholarships appear in 04-scholarships. Universities also offer partial tuition scholarships for strong students, especially at postgraduate level.

### Is this for you?
Ireland suits students aiming at careers in tech, finance or pharma, who want an English-speaking country in Europe with moderate costs and a clear path to residence. It suits proactive, city-clear students.

## Germany

Tagline: Tuition-free education at public universities, a booming job market, and an 18-month post-study work visa.

### Overview
Germany offers something almost nowhere else does: tuition-free education at most public universities for international students. You pay a semester fee that covers administration and often your public transport pass, but not tuition. Combined with a strong, demand-driven job market and an 18-month post-study work visa, this makes Germany one of the best value destinations on earth for serious students.

### Intakes
- Main: October (Semester 1)
- Secondary: April (Semester 2)

### Study costs (approximate, annual, EUR)
- Public university tuition: ~0 (semester fee EUR 300 – 500)
- Living costs: EUR 11,000 – 13,000

### Work rights
- Up to 140 full days or 280 half days per year
- No weekly-hour cap during term in most states

### Post-study work
The 18-month residence permit to seek work allows graduates to take any job while looking for skilled employment matching the qualification. Skilled worker visas (Blue Card routes) then lead to residence.

### Visa route
- Level of visa: National Visa (Type D)
- Proof of funds required: Yes (blocked account)
- Health insurance required: Yes
- Interview: Often required
- Typical processing: 6–12 weeks

### Housing
Studentenwerk dormitories are cheap but competitive; apply the day the cycle opens. Shared flats (WG) are the most common urban solution. Blocked-account documentation is a key early step.

### Scholarships
DAAD is the flagship and appears in 04-scholarships. German universities also offer a range of partial scholarships funded by foundations once admitted.

### Is this for you?
Germany suits disciplined, independent students — usually in engineering, IT, business or natural sciences — who can handle a demanding system in exchange for near-zero tuition. It requires German or strong English and real self-management.

## France

Tagline: World-class engineering and business schools, affordable tuition at public universities, and a clear post-study work pathway.

### Overview
France runs the world's strongest engineering and business school network alongside affordable public universities. Tuition at public institutions is heavily subsidised for international students, and the post-study work pathway is genuinely open. Paris and the major cities offer an unmatched mix of culture, industry and global employers.

### Intakes
- Main: September
- Secondary: January (for some programmes)

### Study costs (approximate, annual, EUR)
- Public university tuition: EUR 3,000 – 7,000 (check current subsidies)
- Living costs: EUR 10,000 – 15,000 (Paris higher)

### Work rights
- Up to 20 hours/week during study
- Full-time during official breaks

### Post-study work
Graduates with a master's degree or higher can receive a one-year post-study residence authorisation to seek work, renewable in committee upon finding a job with a salary above a set threshold. This feeds into the longer-term talent pathway.

### Visa route
- Level of visa: Long-stay Student Visa
- Proof of funds required: Yes
- Health insurance required: Yes
- Interview: Occasionally required
- Typical processing: 4–8 weeks

### Housing
CROUS residences are subsidised but limited; private accommodation fills through regional platforms. Students who secure housing early have a much smoother arrival.

### Scholarships
Eiffel Excellence Scholarships appear in 04-scholarships. French business schools and engineering grandes écoles also award significant merit scholarships on admission.

### Is this for you?
France suits strong students with clear subject direction — especially in engineering, business, design and hospitality — who value affordability with prestige. It rewards those who engage with the French system, including the language for daily life.
